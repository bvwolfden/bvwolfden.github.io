01 · Know the room

THE PEOPLE
BEHIND THE PAIRINGS.

Read the golfer and the partner. The same guy can be a different opponent by the back nine.

Red Team · the opposition

The human fairway

Chad

Calm, methodical, consistent. He beats Brian more often than not because a boring par is still a par. A gummy does not magically turn him into a cartoon villain; he may simply become an even quieter Chad.

Blue’s job

Do not spend your best material on him. Make his partner decide without the comfort of Chad making every call.

“Chad, could you develop one visible weakness? For morale.”
The polished variable

Duffy

Country-club manners without the country-club act. He can shoot 80 or 100, and make either sound like a perfectly pleasant walk. He plays to his partner's temperature: orderly with Chad, increasingly conversational with Ryan.

Blue’s job

Watch the scoring, not the charm. If the round gets social, keep Blue's safe ball boringly in play.

“Duffy, do you two have a plan, or just excellent commentary?”
The serious shape-shifter

Brett

He treats golf, pickleball and biking as if a trophy committee might arrive. With Chad, he can be exacting; with Ryan, he can become the entertainment. With Duffy, the gears have historically failed to mesh.

Blue’s job

Never tell him he is rattled. Let Red supply two opinions, then ask which one made the trip to the tee.

“Brett, which version of the plan survived the cart ride?”
The accidental closer

Ryan Schwartz

Looks like he came for a good time, which he did. He also played golf in high school, gets strokes and owns a 4–0 singles record. The relaxed act is not proof that he cannot focus; a score that matters can switch the old golfer back on.

Blue’s job

Keep it funny without announcing that he has to carry Red. If he starts reading every putt, retire the joke and play the number.

“Ryan, save something for the post-round interview.”

Blue Team · the delivery system

The captain with a ceiling

Brian Vukmir

Best raw Blue golfer and inventor of the AI playbook. Can beat Chad any day; can also make one seven feel like a constitutional crisis. Beer and ego are lousy co-captains.

On the course

After a bad hole: name the next safe shot, not the last injustice.

“The model predicted this shot. It did not predict my last one.”
The dependable chaos

Farrell

West Virginia baseball jock, B player, passionate about golf. The swing stays remarkably steady while the jokes become considerably less eligible for print.

On the course

Trade one joke with Duffy or Ryan, then hit the ball. Do not accept an invitation to host their entire show.

“That was a lot of words for a man who still has to hit.”
The quiet quartermaster

Anthony “Ange” Angelini

Former high-school golfer, lifelong responsible adult and the nicest competitive man in the cart. He organizes the trip, keeps the match together and rarely talks shit.

On the course

Be Blue's reset button. If you do say something, wait until the ball stops. The delay is the joke.

“Well, that settles the discussion.”
The net-score heckler

Nate Majewski

Tall, intelligently goofy and improving: roughly 130 a few years ago, now around 100. Singles has not rewarded him yet. In partner play, his strokes can make one ordinary-looking hole deeply inconvenient for Red.

On the course

Own the strokes without apology. Get the room's attention once, deliver the line, then let the scorecard finish it.

“I'd like to thank the handicap committee for believing in me.”

BLUE PLAYER ROLES

FIRST THREE HOLES

First three holes: agree on the safe player before each tee. Keep one Blue ball alive; take the flag only once the other is secure. Do not invite Chad into a trash-talk contest he has no interest in entering.

If Brett keeps checking Chad, offer one harmless observation about the approval process, then stop. If Brett is locked in, abandon the bit and play straightforward golf.

THE CLOSE

Late: play the cup state, not Brian's mood. A halve can be a very adult result. Ange's quiet ball still counts even when nobody made a speech about it.

“Chad brought a plan. Brett, did you get a copy?”

03 · Extracurricular scouting report

BEER. GUMMIES.
MUSHROOMS. MATH.

Golf has enough variables already. These are jokes and things to watch, not a forecast that anyone’s score will collapse.

BEER · THE ROOM GETS LOUDER

“By beer three, every carry looks shorter. The lake has not been drinking.”

Duffy and Ryan can turn a partner match into a podcast nobody subscribed to. Let them host. Farrell gets one joke; Ange keeps the score. Brian does not let an IPA choose his next club. If the cart gets funnier while Red's decisions get worse, Blue keeps playing. If their golf stays sharp, retire the theory immediately.

GUMMIES · CALM IS NOT A WEAKNESS

“Chad with a gummy is still Chad. Fucking unfair.”

The temptation is to treat a relaxed opponent as a distracted opponent. Chad is still methodical; Ryan's 4–0 singles record says his easygoing posture can hide a switch. Watch the time spent on the putt and the score he writes, not how chilled he looks in the cart.

LINE FOR THE CARTIf Chad gets any calmer, we will have to call this a wellness retreat.
MUSHROOMS · THE COURSE REMAINS LITERAL

“The trees may be telling you something. I believe it is provisional.”

This is a running joke, not a scouting report about anyone. The fairway can acquire a personality, the green can become a philosophical question, and the out-of-bounds stakes remain depressingly literal. If a read takes longer than a TED Talk, Blue picks a number and gets on with it.

LINE FOR THE CARTThe green already has six breaks. We do not need an interpretive one.

04 · Three-day reality check

WATCH THE CARD.
NOT THE LEGEND.

The recorded tables show pair histories, not a verified beer-to-bogey curve. Observe the trip as it unfolds.

FRIDAY · FIND THE TRUE VERSION

Start with the documented pair strengths. Vuk / Farrell is 3–0; Chad / Brett is Red’s 64.3% hammer. Watch whether Duffy follows his partner into serious golf or a talk show, and whether Ryan is joking or quietly scoring.

SATURDAY · CHANGE ONE THING

Compare actual front and back nines. If Duffy and Ryan get louder and looser, do not join the podcast. If Brett becomes sharper beside Chad, stop calling him vulnerable. If Brian makes a seven, Ange is authorized to declare the investigation closed.

SUNDAY · RESPECT THE CLOSER

Ryan is 4–0 in recorded singles; Nate is 0–3 against him. Do not hand Red that draw to prove a point. The captain reads all four matches, not just the one in his cart. The after-hours jokes end the moment the cup needs a number.

ONE SAFE BALL. ONE ATTACK BALL. NO TED TALK FROM THE MUSHROOMS.
